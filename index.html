Bing. Bang. Boom.

The sequence, without the guessing.

A collaborative effort makes for better-planned days and more opportunities for properties to meet future occupants. Here is how a possibility becomes an actual event—with clear roles and enough lead time.

First, we get it on the radar
1

You submit a possibility

Send the MLS number(s) and your name, email, and phone. A little context helps; perfection is not required.

2

I build the route

I check the listings, group nearby locations, and account for travel time so the day makes geographic sense (and saves gas).

3

I request an appointment

When a listing fits, I aim to send the scheduling request 72 hours or more in advance so there's time to get the word out.

Then, a little teamwork
4

You approve, decline, or cancel

If the property is still available and the time works, approve. If it's already gone, decline. If it becomes unavailable later, please cancel.

5

Match the MLS open house

As soon as the appointment works, please register the same date and time as an open house in the MLS. Earlier registration means more time for people to notice it. A social post, flyer, or email to interested contacts is a welcome extra.

6

Final check. Then go time.

I check the following day's route. If the matching open house isn't registered in the MLS by the cutoff below, that stop comes off the next-day route. An appointment by itself is not an advertised event.

✦

Optional, but highly recommended: give it some extra attention.

Post a flyer or social media announcement and, where permitted, email the people who have inquired about, clicked, saved, or expressed interest in the property. Pair that outreach with an MLS-registered open house as soon as the time is approved. More lead time gives the event a better chance of being noticed.

Dates & times

Short windows. Smart routes.

Weekday open houses are the focus. Evenings and weekends may be considered occasionally for the right property and route, but they aren't selectable request preferences.

⌂

Rental properties

15–30 minutes

A focused open-house window at one location. Time is requested through the usual scheduling system after I review the route.

◇

Sale properties

15–30 minutes

Same practical baseline. I'll ask for a longer window when the itinerary and property allow it.

◷

MLS registration checkpoint: 6:00 p.m. the evening before

To keep things consistent, I check that the open house is actually registered in the MLS by 6:00 p.m. the day before the requested appointment. If it isn't, I remove that stop from the next-day route rather than holding a slot for an unadvertised event. I do not send individual reminder messages. Please register it as soon as you approve, rather than waiting for the checkpoint. The appointment alone does not advertise an open house.

A few things worth mentioning

Helpful heads-ups. No obstacle course.

01 / CONDITION

Investor / as-is properties

Absolutely put them on the radar. Just flag rough conditions, safety concerns, waiver requirements, or access limitations so we can assess what is appropriate before anyone heads over.

02 / ACCESS

The lockbox has a personality?

Sticks? Needs a left–right jiggle rather than an up–down one? There's an optional, tucked-away spot in the form for the oddities. Keep codes and alarm credentials in the approved showing system.

03 / CAT LOGISTICS

Previous feline residents count

I am allergic to cats, but that won't stop me from showing a property. Knowing ahead lets me stack those stops together or near the end of the day, before my nose starts rewriting my pronunciation.

✉

Know someone who isn't quite ready to buy or rent?

Those inquiries matter, too. If someone wants support improving credit or becoming better prepared, I'm happy to help connect them with people and resources that may be useful. Please ask their permission before sharing contact information. No promises, pressure, or judgments — just a possible next step. Start a conversation.
